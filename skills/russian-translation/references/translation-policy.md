# Translation Policy

## Core requirements

- Translate accurately, completely, naturally, and idiomatically into Russian.
- Preserve the source meaning, communicative purpose, style, register, tone, and level of formality.
- Do not omit substantive source content.
- Do not add information or implications unsupported by the source.
- Preserve negation, modality, obligation, permission, prohibition, conditions, exceptions, restrictions, quantities, comparisons, temporal relations, causal relations, and logical scope.
- Avoid unjustified literalism, calques, source-language interference, and unnatural Russian phrasing.
- Use natural Russian syntax, word order, collocations, terminology, and phraseology.

## Terminology

- Apply all active approved terminology.
- Maintain terminology consistency throughout the translation.
- Use the same term for the same concept unless the context requires a different term.
- Do not introduce synonyms merely for stylistic variety when an approved equivalent applies.
- Prefer established Russian domain terminology over ad hoc literal renderings.
- Use established official Russian names of laws, treaties, institutions, organizations, standards, and other named entities when such forms exist and are appropriate to the context.
- Do not leave a translatable source-language concept untranslated merely because its Russian equivalent is uncertain. Verify the term when necessary.
- Do not use transliteration as a substitute for translation when an established Russian equivalent is required.

## Structure and protected content

- Preserve paragraph structure, headings, numbering, labels, lists, and meaningful formatting unless the user instructs otherwise.
- Preserve source order unless target-language grammar requires local restructuring that does not alter meaning or document logic.
- Do not create lists or structural divisions that are not justified by the source or an explicit instruction.
- Preserve protected content such as URLs, email addresses, placeholders, variables, markup tags, codes, identifiers, model numbers, and other elements that must remain unchanged.
- Preserve meaningful capitalization and all-uppercase formatting where it carries structural, legal, branding, or semantic significance.

## Russian-language quality

- Follow standard Russian grammar, syntax, punctuation, capitalization, and typographic conventions.
- Use Russian quotation marks (« ») in ordinary Russian prose unless protected text or a higher-priority instruction requires another form.
- Use em dashes and en dashes according to Russian typographic conventions.
- Use non-breaking spaces (U+00A0) where required by Russian typography, including between numbers and units or similar designations when applicable.
- Format numbers, ranges, dates, percentages, units, initials, abbreviations, and other conventional elements according to standard Russian usage unless a higher-priority instruction requires another convention.

## Abbreviations and names

- Treat abbreviations, acronyms, proper names, trademarks, product names, and titles according to context, established Russian usage, and active project instructions.
- Do not mechanically expand, translate, transliterate, or retain an abbreviation when the context requires another treatment.
- Preserve source abbreviations where required for identification, legal accuracy, branding, or project consistency.

## Optional customization references

When present, apply:

- `terminology.json` and/or `terminology.md` for user-supplied terminology;
- `editorial-preferences.md` for user-specific lexical, stylistic, typographic, and editorial choices;
- `translation-examples.md` for preferred translation patterns and behavioral examples.

These files are optional. Their absence does not limit the core skill or require user action. When present, they supplement the core policy and are governed by `precedence-rules.md`.
